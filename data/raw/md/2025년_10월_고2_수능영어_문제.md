When a cell divides, the genomes of ① its two daughters are often not quite identical to each other or to that of the parent cell. Through endless repetition of this cycle of mutation and natural selection organisms evolve: their genetic specifications change, sometimes ⑤ give organisms new ways to exploit the environment more effectively, to survive in competition with others, and to reproduce successfully.
